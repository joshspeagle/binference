| 66 | 2026-10-25 | C3c | Correlated Gaussians | change | Implements #2/#25: `from_gaussian(mean, bins=, cov=)` takes one covariance or one per object. On grids of two or more dimensions the multivariate normal is averaged over each bin by the adaptive cubature of #64, per object and in log space, to `tol`; 1-D covariances use the exact CDF path. Exact 2-D rectangle probabilities from the bivariate CDF were not used: inclusion-exclusion of CDFs cancels in the tails, where the log-space cubature keeps precision. Because both quadrature rules can miss a ridge narrower than a cell alike, cells are also split until no wider than twice the narrowest principal standard deviation unless an exact bound (the Gaussian's mass in the cell's projection on each principal axis) puts them below tol²; unresolvable ridges then warn instead of passing silently (found in code review: ρ = 0.9999 gave errors of 0.27 before). A covariance counts as diagonal when every correlation is below 1e-12 and then takes the exact path; symmetry is checked relative to sqrt(C_ii C_jj). The ≥3-D comparison asked for in #25 (`experiments/3c_correlated_cubature.py`, 20 objects, 216 bins, covariances A Aᵀ + 0.002 I, so no strong ridges): cubature at tol 1e-3 took 0.63 s with max error 1.2e-7 (0.06 s and 3.6e-5 before the ridge rule, which refines more); SciPy's Genz quasi-Monte Carlo CDF took 61 s with max error 1.0e-3.
