5 · Drawing rules

The mark's language becomes the rule for every figure and explainer on the site.

  1. Uncertainty is drawn, never hidden. A population is shown as its posterior draws (6–8 pale fills, or a band), with the mean as one line. A bare histogram with no spread is never the answer.
  2. Indigo is the population, coral is an object. Draws, bins and estimates are indigo; data points, samples and single objects are coral; the truth is an ink dashed line. Coral marks nothing else.
  3. Bins are steps. Anything binned is drawn as square steps, never smoothed; continuous curves are only for what is continuous (a likelihood, a truth).
  4. Square and flat. Corners square (2 px at most on controls); no gradients, no shadows, no glows; hairline rules, not boxes.
  5. Words before colour. Direct labels over legends; hue is never the only signal (fill vs outline, dot vs line, dashed vs solid); every figure reads in greyscale.
  6. Readable on a phone. Figure text is at least 12 px at 390 px wide; wide figures get a narrow version; every figure has a caption and real alt text.

Concepts / Population and objects

Population and objects

binference infers two things at once: the population the objects came from, and each object's value given that population.

In plain wordsAn object measured badly borrows strength from the others. Its estimate moves toward values that are common in the population.

Shrinkage

The posterior of one object is its likelihood times the population fractions. Where the population is high, the posterior gains mass.

python
import binference
result = binference.infer(lk)
post = result.individual(3)   # one object's posterior
output
bin       mean    q16     q84
[0.4, 0.5) 0.31   0.22    0.40
WarningPosteriors computed under another prior are not likelihoods. Pass the interim prior.

7 · Home page

The hero is the mark itself, live: its draws keep resampling, so the b never holds still but its shape does. Under reduced motion it stays still. Three paths start from the data the reader has.

Infer the population, then every object.

binference turns noisy measurements, posteriors, samples or classifier outputs into a binned population with honest uncertainty, and gives each object's value under it.

Quick startWhat it does
Measurements with errorsGaussian or any likelihood per objectPosteriors or samplesComputed under some other priorA classifier or binned countsScores, labels, a response matrix
