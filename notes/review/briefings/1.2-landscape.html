The landscape, revisited

The survey behind pastry (reports/Population inference software landscape.md, 22 Oct 2026): what each field already has, what pastry must match or beat, which recommendations the code follows, and which ones we haven't discussed yet.

§1

One model, at least ten names

Every tool in the survey maximises or samples the same likelihood, $\prod_i \sum_k L_{ik}\rho_k$. The survey's headline: reinvented everywhere, packaged nowhere. Maintained tools are domain-locked, point-estimate-only, or in R/Julia; the ones with honest uncertainty are paper code.

The teaching device we already use (2.1 §6) comes from here: stacking is one EM step, IBU with early stopping is truncated EM, and pastry runs the iteration to convergence with uncertainty.

§2

Field by field: what to match or beat

Tap a field. "Bar" is the existing tool pastry has to be better than for that community to switch.

Photo-z n(z) replace

Bar: RAIL's VarInfStackSummarizer, the only maintained general tool: mean-field VB, flat Dir(1), PDFs evaluated at grid points rather than integrated, no interim-prior correction. HSC Y3 puts its under-coverage at about 20%. Exact samplers (Leistedt's Gibbs, CHIPPR, frankenz) are stale.

To win: exact posterior, interim prior divided out, a RAIL plug-in and qp / CosmoSIS / nz_prior exports. A 2026 KiDS analysis still subsampled to 10⁵ galaxies because the Gibbs sampler was "too slow": speed matters here.

Stellar populations, CMDs, Hess diagrams engineering bar

Bar: extreme deconvolution (Gaussian noise, maximum likelihood, no uncertainty) in practice; StarFormationHistories.jl (Julia) as the engineering exemplar: Poisson template fits, MAP + Hessian, HMC, CI and coverage reports.

To win: Leistedt & Hogg's 1.4 M-star TGAS run is the scale benchmark; correlated Gaussians (#25) and masked 2-D grids (#19) are table stakes.

Occurrence rates package it

Bar: paper code only. Foreman-Mackey, Hogg & Morton (2014) had the full extension set: completeness, interim-prior weights, GP smoothness, Poisson rates.

To win: exactly that, packaged and tested; completeness from injections (#10, #30); rates per draw (#9).

Gravitational waves high bar

Bar: the best-engineered neighbour. gwpopulation (Monte Carlo variance accounting, injections, NumPy/CuPy/JAX); PixelPop and gppop run NUTS over 10⁴–8×10⁴ smooth bins on GPUs.

To win: pastry won't replace GW tools, but should match their Monte Carlo accounting (our #31 check is their variance cut) and say plainly that likelihoods supplied on the bins carry no Monte Carlo noise at all. Their bin counts are the scale target for smooth priors, which needs the JAX backend.

Particle-physics unfolding vacant niche

Bar: RooUnfold (C++/ROOT); PyUnfold frozen since 2018; PyFBU, the one Bayesian package, dead with Theano. No HEP tool takes a per-event likelihood matrix.

To win: fully Bayesian unfolding with response uncertainty inferred jointly (#27, #28), fine bins with coarse functionals (Kuusela). The survey cites a warning that estimated responses "can inadvertently regularize the problem", which #28 addresses.

Empirical Bayes (statistics) references

Bar: mixsqp, REBayes (needs MOSEK), ashr, deconvolveR in R; Empirikos.jl in Julia, the only frequentist intervals for functionals of g. Python has two alphas.

To win: a Python home with uncertainty. Their solvers and bundled datasets are golden values for our tests, which we don't use yet.

Quantification (machine learning) API to learn from

Bar: QuaPy: EMQ, BayesianMAPLS with NUTS, (estimate, region) pairs, and likelihood tempering tuned on validation coverage. Inputs are features + an sklearn classifier, not likelihoods.

To win: route C with the response inferred (#29), the label-shift test, selection. BayesianCC (Ziegler & Czyż) already learns a confusion matrix jointly with the prevalences: prior art for route B under #28.

Genetics and genomics vacant in Python

Bar: rubias (archived from CRAN, Feb 2026), SourceTracker2 (unreleased since 2016), salmon/kallisto (equivalence classes = our compress()).

To win: baseline-sample uncertainty is the same machinery as #28; rubias's "reporting-unit bias" is the Dirichlet-on-unequal-groups issue we parked for 3.1.

Neural deconvolution later

Single-paper code, no calibrated uncertainty. On gridded inputs a flexible density needs no IWAE machinery and keeps exact uncertainty, so flows belong to sample-only, multi-dimensional problems, later.

§5

What the survey raises that we haven't discussed

  1. Overdispersion. The Dirichlet–multinomial treats objects as independent draws from one ρ, so its width is shot noise only. For a survey field, sample variance (cosmic variance, clustering) can be an order of magnitude larger. Options in the survey: a concentration rescaling (DES's 3sDir), per-field layers $\rho_f \sim \mathrm{Dir}(\lambda\bar\rho)$, or a logistic-normal with a simulation-derived covariance. This also clarifies what ρ means (2.1 §8: the population vs this sample).
  2. Calibration of the per-object PDFs. Over- or under-estimated errors bias the deconvolution ("overestimated errors cause excessive deconvolution"). QuaPy tempers the likelihood on validation coverage. A tempering or error-scaling hyperparameter, fitted or checked, would extend principle 10 ("every uncertain input is modelled") to the likelihoods themselves.
  3. Interim prior when it's implicit. For machine-learning photo-z the interim prior is the training set's distribution, never written down. Quantification estimates it as the mean held-out posterior. A density constructor built from held-out posteriors (e.g. Density.from_heldout(...)) would fit #26.
  4. Samples: "50 per star was only slightly worse than 10⁵". This is Hogg, Myers & Bovy's finding, which seems to contradict our "S ≳ N". The two are consistent: the error grows like N/S and with the mismatch between population and interim prior, so a small catalogue or a population close to the interim tolerates few samples. Worth stating in the docs with the #31 check doing the deciding.
  5. Scale and memory. TGAS on a 100×100 grid needed about 109 GB as a dense matrix. Chunked or streamed likelihoods, and the GPU path for smooth priors, decide whether "blazing fast" (#33) holds at 10⁷–10⁸ objects.
  6. Run control. Checkpoint files and a generator interface (emcee, nautilus) for long runs.
  7. Interoperability. Exports to each field's format (qp/CosmoSIS for cosmology, popsummary for GW, histograms with covariance for HEP) are how a field-neutral package (#37) gets adopted field by field.
§6

frankenz, in one paragraph

The survey audited your own frankenz samplers. The data-augmentation Gibbs sampler is exact. The pairwise Metropolis "population" sampler is not (missing Hastings factor; sparse bins collapse to about 1e-13) and the "flexible reference" step belongs to no joint model (Geweke |z| = 70). The largest errors came from inputs: posteriors used as likelihoods biased fractions by up to 12.6 posterior SD, and bin integrals in place of bin averages by up to 28 SD on unequal bins. pastry ported only the Gibbs sampler and built the input conventions around those two mistakes; the coherent replacements for the flexible reference (power prior, hierarchical reference) are still to come and now share machinery with #28.
